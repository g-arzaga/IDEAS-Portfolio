IDEA220

ECOLOGICAL DESIGN

Ritual Objects

My final project centered around the ritual my family performs after surfing in Pacifica, California. The purpose of the ritual is preservation of gear and heat, both also reliant on the other.

The designed objects serve to make the ritual more efficient. This can mean a faster process for a single person or the tools for an assembly line style conduction of the ritual. In construction, the objects would only utilize materials already part of the ritual or beach waste.

Concepts and Sketches

I began my concept map by creating sketches of the important objects used in my ritual then categorized them into groups.

Elements:

  • Water
  • Heat
  • Durability
  • Capacity
  • Repurpose

Themes:

  • Protection
  • Preservation
  • Fun in danger

I ultimately selected three processes to focus on that are integral to my family's surfing day ritual:

  1. Rinsing off our bodies at the beach using hot water jugs brought from home
  2. Rinsing off our wetsuits in our backyard by dunking them in water from the hose
  3. Wringing out the excess water from the wetsuits and hanging them to dry on the playhouse

Hot Water Sleeve & Carrier

Before leaving the house to go surfing, we fill up empty juice containers with hot water to avoid using the cold showers at the beach.

In this design, soda can rings and fish nets have been gone from common ocean pollutants to a carrier for the water jug, reframing their functions and relationship to this ocean-based ritual.

I was inspired by my dad's idea to use an old, worn wetsuit to create a thermally insulated sleeve. In this way, a wetsuit's lifespan can be extended to keep our bodies warm even after getting out of the ocean.

As a physical representation, I knit a sleeve to fit around a soda can to tie in the carrier inspiration and theme of heat preservation.

Food Pedal Rinse Bucket

Arriving home after surfing, rinse the salt water out our wetsuits with fresh hose water to maintain the material's longevity

Wetsuits can get very heavy when completely saturated with water, so I designed a foot pedal system to minimize possible back pain from continually leaning over a bucket.

It repurposes a surfboard leash for the pulley and a fin as the pedal. The system is designed to fit over our existing rinse bucket and make this step easier and quicker.

Wetsuit Wringing Station: Playhouse Add On

The last step is to wring out the excess water from the wetsuit so that it can dry overnight. We typically hang the wetsuits over the top of a playhouse from our childhood.

I incorporated window openings on opposite sides of the playhouse as westuit entry and exits for the wringer. An old surfboard is fitted as a surface between the windows, with a slanted drainage channel underneath.

A twisted belt connects two non-absorbent rollers so that turning them with a lever causes them to converge in the middle. This way, a wetsuit entering the wringer will be fed toward the exit while squeezing water out of the material.
